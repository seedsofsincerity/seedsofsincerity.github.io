How it grows

From sentiment to sprout

A card you design yourself, and a gift that keeps going long after the occasion.

  1. Sow your sentiment

    Pick the paper and up to three flowers or herbs. The seeds are pressed into the paper, so you can see tiny specks where they sit.

  2. Write it sincerely

    Add a name and a message from the heart. Ballpoint and pencil work well on seed paper. Skip wet markers, which can soak through.

  3. Plant and watch

    Lay the card under about ¼ inch (6 mm) of soil, keep it damp, and give it sun. Seedlings usually show up in one to three weeks.

    Questions, sincerely

    Before you plant

    When is the best time to plant the card?

    Plant annuals like marigold, zinnia, cosmos and sunflower after your last spring frost. Forget-me-nots do well sown in late summer or fall for spring blooms.

    Will the rose really grow?

    It can, but roses are slow from seed. Expect leafy growth the first season and flowers in the second. Pair it with a faster flower like cosmos or sweet pea so the card gives you blooms sooner.

    Can it grow in a pot?

    Yes. Fill a pot with damp potting mix, lay the card on top, cover with a thin layer of soil, and keep it moist. Pick shorter varieties like marigold, forget-me-not or basil for small pots.

    Can I write on it and mail it?

    Yes. Use a ballpoint pen or pencil. The card is flat and light, so it travels like any other greeting card. Keep it dry until it is planted.

    What goes on the back of the card?

    Planting steps, the seeds inside, and an optional note from you. Choose a note in step 5 and flip the preview to see it.

    How long until I see flowers?

    Seedlings usually appear in one to three weeks. Most annual flowers bloom about eight to fourteen weeks after sprouting, depending on the variety and the weather.
